Project 04 2025 Concept · Prototype

Haile

A holographic AI companion designed to help early-stage dementia patients maintain independence — tested with real users using Wizard of Oz methodology.

01 — Concept

AI that feels like someone, not something.

HAIlie was conceptualised as a hologram-powered AI device that combines artificial intelligence with an intuitive visual interface to provide personalised daily assistance, real-time safety alerts, and adaptive learning — specifically designed for adults aged 60+ with mild cognitive impairments.

The core insight: existing voice assistants like Alexa and Siri feel impersonal and intimidating to elderly users, particularly those with early-stage dementia. A holographic presence — something you can see and talk to — is fundamentally different. More approachable. More human.

02 — Research

A gap in eldercare technology.

Research revealed that by 2050, one in six people globally will be over 65, creating urgent demand for accessible assistive technology. Studies indicated that elderly people are 40% more likely to engage with devices featuring audiovisual elements — which led the team to explore holographic interfaces as the more approachable alternative.

The target audience was defined as adults aged 60+ with mild cognitive impairments and their caregivers, who need reliable tools for managing daily routines, staying connected, and ensuring safety without feeling surveilled or patronised.

03 — Design

A companion, not a device.

HAIlie was designed with user-centred principles at the core — a customisable holographic companion that could guide users through tasks like cooking, remembering appointments, and medication schedules. Key features included room-specific sensors for safety monitoring, a caregiver dashboard for routine insights and irregularity notifications, and smart home integration.

User testing revealed critical nuances: the AI should use suggestive rather than commanding tones, personalisation options (such as projecting familiar faces or authority figures like doctors) were highly valued, and respecting user autonomy in every interaction was non-negotiable.

04 — Development

Cardboard, glass, and a phone screen.

The team built a low-fidelity prototype using a cardboard box fitted with angled glass to reflect a phone screen, simulating the holographic effect. User testing was conducted with four participants aged 68–78 using accessibility testing, Wizard of Oz methodology, and think-aloud protocols.

The prototype demonstrated core functionality: the holographic projection, verbally responsive AI, and the sensor integration concept. Testing validated that while current holographic technology remains premature for consumer markets, the concept points in a genuinely promising direction for eldercare — one that future generations will be far more receptive to.
